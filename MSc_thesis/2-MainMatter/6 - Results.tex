\section{General comparison between 4G and 5G}
\label{sec:4g-vs-5g}

This section presents a comparison of the 4G baseline against the 5G network across the three test rounds described in Section~\ref{sec:test-methodology}, focusing on the evolution of performance metrics. Because the 5G configuration changed between rounds, namely the TDD structure and the \texttt{rx\_gain} setting, these results are presented as a progression rather than a single snapshot, tracking how each change affected throughput at each location and in each direction. As will become clear over the following sections, no single round can be presented as the definitive 5G result: the configuration that performs best at short range is not the same as the one that performs best at long range, a trade-off examined in more detail later in this chapter.

In Figures \ref{fig:tp-tcp-dl} through \ref{fig:tp-udp-ul}, the mean throughput results for TCP and UDP in both downlink and uplink directions are presented for each test location, with black lines representing the range between Min/Max throughput values between each repetition for that combination. The figures mainly illustrate the performance differences between 4G and 5G across the three rounds of testing, with the results showing that the 5G network can outperform the 4G baseline in certain configurations and locations, but also underperform in others.

% --- 2. Uplink airtime and TDD decomposition ---------------------------------------
% THE ARGUMENT: the 3:1 pattern was a hard structural cap on uplink, independent of
% signal quality. Rebalancing to 2:2 lifted the cap and paid for it in downlink airtime.
% This is the cleanest quantitative result in the dataset, and -- unlike the raw
% throughput numbers -- it is measured on both sides and survives the SNR confound below.
%
% LEAD WITH THE DECOMPOSITION, NOT THE FIGURE (FINDINGS.md S2). Separate the two factors
% by counting grants rather than Mb/s:
%   - Factor A, airtime. 3:1 gives one uplink slot per 2.5 ms = a 400/s ceiling; the
%     measured rate is 372-394 grants/s. 4G FDD gets one uplink subframe per ms, ~975/s.
%     That is a 2.5x structural penalty no amount of signal quality can recover.
%   - Factor B, link budget. Bits per grant. At 2 m the 5G and 4G payloads are effectively
%     identical (13 634 vs 13 429 bits); at 10 m the 5G payload collapses 8.7x.
%   Multiply them: 2.5 x 1.0 = 2.6x deficit at 2 m (measured 2.59x), 2.5 x 8.7 = 21.5x at
%   10 m (measured 21.5x). The decomposition is exact at both distances. This is what
%   earns the section its own heading.
%
% THE REBALANCE DELIVERED EXACTLY WHAT WAS PREDICTED (FINDINGS_RERUN_2026-08-02.md).
%   2:2 gives two uplink slots per 2.5 ms = 800/s, and the scheduler grants the full
%   800/s at both 2 m and 10 m. The x2 airtime factor is therefore measured, not inferred.
%   Airtime also predicts downlink x0.75 (4 -> 3 DL slots); observed TCP DL is x0.56 at A
%   and x0.44 at C, i.e. downlink lost somewhat more than airtime alone accounts for.
%
% WHAT FIGURE~\ref{fig:tdd-structure} SHOWS. Only one variable moves per step: TDD between
% the first two points, rx_gain between the last two. Say that explicitly, or a reader in
% a section titled "TDD decomposition" will attribute the second step to airtime as well.
%   DL/UL ratio, TCP:   A: 5.84 -> 1.32 -> 0.89     C: 17.55 -> 0.39 -> 2.48
%   At A the two directions converge monotonically and finish nearly balanced (22.4 DL vs
%   25.2 UL) -- the best balance anywhere in the dataset. At C they do not: rx_gain 60
%   gives back most of the uplink Round 2 recovered (11.60 -> 2.77 Mb/s, a 4.2x
%   regression) and the ratio swings past 1 to 2.48. So write "close at short range", not
%   "close". The Location C behaviour is not a blemish on the result -- it is the rx_gain
%   trade-off that Section~\ref{sec:uplink-quality-rxgain} builds to, and this figure is
%   its clearest evidence. Forward-reference it rather than explaining it here.
%
% CONFOUND THAT HAS TO BE STATED (FINDINGS_RERUN_2026-08-02.md). Between Rounds 1 and 2,
% at identical gain settings, uplink SNR rose +5.4 dB at A and +22.4 dB at C -- documented
% and still unexplained. So the x19.9 uplink gain at C is mostly NOT airtime, which alone
% predicts x2.0. Do not present the Round 1 -> 2 throughput change as a clean measurement
% of the TDD effect. The grants/s counting above is the part that survives, because
% grants/s is independent of SNR: the confound lives entirely in bits-per-grant.
%
% OPTIONAL SECOND FIGURE -- the measured slot map (data_v2/tdd_airtime.csv; plot-ready in
% data_v2/fig_tdd_tradeoff.xlsx, sheet SlotMap). PUSCH appears only in slot 4 under 3:1
% and in slots 3-4 under 2:2: the structural ceiling straight from the scheduler's own
% counters, and the one piece of evidence the confound cannot touch. Three traps:
%   (a) use the slot POSITIONS, not the PRB magnitudes -- the 3:1 unit was an uplink-only
%       run while the 2:2 unit aggregates every test at that location, so the PRB totals
%       (58.5 vs 42.1) are not a capacity ratio and must not be quoted as one;
%   (b) for the same reason the 3:1 PDSCH column is near-zero from absent traffic, not
%       from unavailable slots;
%   (c) Round 1 has no airtime data at all -- its logs carry no Scheduler-cell METRICS
%       lines -- which is why the slot map uses the Round 3 pair (same location, same
%       gain, same session) rather than the Round 1 vs Round 2 pair used in the figure.
%
% ALSO STATE:
%   - Location B is absent from the figure: Round 2 at B is the unusable metal-stand data.
%   - The Round 3 TDD isolation run (R3_5G_C_TDD31) cannot settle this. It is TCP uplink
%     only, so it has no downlink leg and cannot show a trade-off at all, and it ran at
%     rx_gain 60, which desensitises Location C. Report it as inconclusive rather than as
%     a contradiction, and flag "redo at rx_gain 70" as future work.
%   - The first configuration is n=1 per condition (Round 1); the other two are n=3.
